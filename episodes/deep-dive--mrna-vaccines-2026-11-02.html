Deep Dive · How mRNA Vaccines Work

Download MP3 · Plain-text transcript

Key points

  • mRNA is a strictly temporary, cytoplasmic instruction that degrades quickly and cannot integrate into cellular DNA.
  • Manufacturing uses cell-free in vitro transcription (IVT), enabling rapid vaccine redesign (weeks) by simply swapping the DNA template.
  • Modified nucleosides like N1-methylpseudouridine (m1Ψ) hide synthetic RNA from innate immune sensors and increase translation efficiency.
  • The 2P mutation chemically locks the viral antigen (e.g., spike protein) in its prefusion shape, ensuring the immune system targets the most effective conformation.
  • Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) use ionizable lipids to safely deliver fragile mRNA into cells, changing charge to escape the acidic endosome and simultaneously acting as an inflammatory adjuvant.
  • The platform uniquely forces the cell to produce antigens internally, priming both CD8⁺ killer T-cells (via MHC class I) and CD4⁺ helper T-cells (via MHC class II).
  • Phase 3 trials showed ~95% efficacy against symptomatic COVID-19, though protection against mild infection wanes, while cellular immunity offers more durable protection against severe disease.
  • The technology frontier includes self-amplifying RNA for lower doses and personalized cancer vaccines targeting unique tumor neoantigens.

Pearls

  • mRNA vaccines cannot alter your DNA; the mRNA never enters the nucleus and human cells lack the machinery for reverse transcription or integration.
  • Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) are not inert delivery vehicles; they are actively inflammatory adjuvants that contribute to local immune stimulation and common reactogenicity (sore arm, fever).
  • The term "95% efficacy" refers to a 95% relative risk reduction compared to an unvaccinated placebo group, not that 5% of vaccinated individuals will get sick.
  • The cell-free in vitro transcription (IVT) process significantly accelerates vaccine development and redesign, allowing for rapid adaptation to new pathogens or variants.
  • Dual antigen presentation by MHC class I and II molecules means mRNA vaccines stimulate both killer T-cells and helper T-cells, providing a robust and comprehensive immune response.

    AnswerA mature mRNA has a 5′ cap, a 5′ untranslated region (UTR), the coding sequence (open reading frame), a 3′ UTR, and a poly(A) tail.
  2. Why is the lipid 'ionizable' rather than permanently charged?
    AnswerAt acidic pH during mixing, the ionizable lipid is positively charged and binds RNA; at body pH it is near-neutral (less toxic); inside the acidic endosome it becomes charged again and helps disrupt the membrane, releasing RNA into the cytoplasm.
  3. Interpret '95% efficacy.'
    AnswerIt means a 95% relative risk reduction versus placebo during the trial window, not that 5% of vaccinated people got sick.
